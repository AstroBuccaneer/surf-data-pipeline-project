LITUYA BAY MEGATSUNAMI — RESEARCH DOCUMENT
==========================================

Event Date: July 9, 1958
Location: Lituya Bay, Alaska, USA
Coordinates: 58.6N, 137.5W

WAVE STATISTICS
---------------
Maximum Wave Height: 1,720 feet (524 meters)
Classification: Megatsunami
Surfable: No
Status: Largest wave ever recorded in human history

CAUSE OF EVENT
--------------
A magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck along the Fairweather Fault
in Southeast Alaska at 10:16 PM local time. The seismic activity
triggered a massive rockslide on the northeast wall of Lituya Bay.
Approximately 90 million tons of rock plunged into the narrow inlet
at the head of the bay generating a megatsunami.

The unique geography of Lituya Bay — a narrow T-shaped fjord —
amplified the wave significantly. The wave stripped trees from
hillsides up to 1,720 feet above sea level on the opposite shore.

GEOLOGICAL CONTEXT
------------------
The Fairweather Fault is a major right-lateral strike-slip fault
running along the coast of Southeast Alaska. It is part of the
Pacific-North American plate boundary system. The fault is capable
of generating large earthquakes due to the rapid movement between
the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate.

RELEVANCE TO SURF PIPELINE PROJECT
------------------------------------
Lituya Bay serves as the scientific upper bound benchmark in the
surf pipeline scoring system. No surfable wave has ever come close
to this magnitude. It represents the theoretical maximum that
seismic activity can generate and is used as a reference point
for understanding the extreme end of wave generation potential.

The seismic cause of this event directly informs the seismic
recurrence scoring component of the surf potential index. Locations
with higher seismic activity near major fault systems have greater
potential for extreme wave generation events.
