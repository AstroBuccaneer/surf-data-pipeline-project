NAZARÉ BIG WAVE RECORD — RESEARCH DOCUMENT
==========================================

Event Date: October 29, 2020
Location: Praia do Norte, Nazaré, Portugal
Coordinates: 39.6N, 9.1W

WAVE STATISTICS
---------------
Maximum Wave Height: 86 feet (26.2 meters)
Classification: Big Wave Surfing Record
Surfer: Sebastian Steudtner (Germany)
Surfable: Yes
Status: Largest wave ever surfed — Guinness World Record

CAUSE OF EVENT
--------------
The Nazaré Canyon is the primary driver of the giant waves at
Nazaré. The canyon extends approximately 170 kilometers into the
Atlantic Ocean reaching depths of 5,000 meters. When Atlantic
swells travel toward the Portuguese coast the canyon funnels and
focuses the wave energy creating waves significantly larger than
surrounding areas.

The canyon acts as a natural amplifier — deep water swells
traveling across the Atlantic Ocean are compressed and focused
as they enter the shallower water near the coast. This bathymetric
effect concentrates the wave energy dramatically increasing wave
height compared to nearby beaches without canyon influence.

SURFER PROFILE
--------------
Sebastian Steudtner is a German professional big wave surfer
who has dedicated his career to chasing and surfing the largest
waves in the world. He has surfed at Nazaré multiple times and
holds the current world record set on October 29 2020.

OCEANOGRAPHIC CONTEXT
----------------------
Atlantic swells that reach Nazaré are typically generated by
powerful low pressure systems in the North Atlantic Ocean.
These storms can generate swells with wave periods of 16-20
seconds and significant wave heights of 8-12 meters in the
open ocean. The Nazaré Canyon amplifies these swells
dramatically as they approach the coast.

RELEVANCE TO SURF PIPELINE PROJECT
------------------------------------
Nazaré serves as the human surfability ceiling benchmark in
the surf pipeline scoring system. It represents the maximum
wave height a human has ever successfully surfed making it
the most relevant benchmark for comparing real surf locations.

Every location in the pipeline is scored as a percentage of
the Nazaré benchmark. A location scoring 37% of Nazaré means
its highest recorded wave was 37% of 86 feet (approximately
32 feet). This gives meaningful context to wave height data
that would otherwise be hard to interpret in isolation.

The Nazaré benchmark may be updated in future versions of the
pipeline if a new world record is set. The Airflow DAG is
designed to check WSL records on a weekly schedule and update
the benchmark automatically if exceeded.
