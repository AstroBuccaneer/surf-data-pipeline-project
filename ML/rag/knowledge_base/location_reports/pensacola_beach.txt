GEOGRAPHIC CONTEXT
------------------
Pensacola Beach sits on a barrier island on the Gulf of Mexico
coast of Florida. The Gulf of Mexico is a semi-enclosed body of
water which limits the fetch — the distance over which wind can
blow to generate waves — compared to open ocean locations.
This fetch limitation means waves are generally smaller than
Atlantic or Pacific coast locations except during major hurricanes.

METEOROLOGICAL DRIVERS
-----------------------
The primary wave driver at Pensacola Beach is hurricane and
tropical storm activity in the Gulf of Mexico. Hurricane season
runs from June through November with peak activity in August
and September. Notable hurricanes that generated significant
wave events include:
- Hurricane Ivan (2004) — Category 4 landfall near Pensacola
- Hurricane Katrina (2005) — Significant surge and wave activity
- Hurricane Sally (2020) — Direct hit on Pensacola Beach

SEISMIC ACTIVITY
----------------
Seismic activity near Pensacola Beach is minimal. Only 6 earthquake
events above magnitude 4.0 were recorded within 500km during the
study period (2000-2023). The maximum magnitude recorded was 5.9.
The Gulf of Mexico region is not near major tectonic plate boundaries
making large seismic wave generation events extremely unlikely.

BEST SURF CONDITIONS
--------------------
Best Season     : Late summer and fall (hurricane season)

Experience Level: Beginner to intermediate
Average Surfable Days: 17.60% of total readings above 1.5m

BEGINNER FRIENDLINESS
---------------------
Pensacola Beach is the most beginner friendly location in the
pipeline due to its consistently small wave heights. The low
average wave height of 0.97 meters and calm Gulf of Mexico
conditions make it ideal for learning to surf. Waves are
manageable most of the year with only occasional large swells
during hurricane season.
