GEOGRAPHIC CONTEXT
------------------
Waikiki sits on the south shore of Oahu in a sheltered bay
protected by a reef system. Hawaii is located in the middle
of the Pacific Ocean surrounded by vast stretches of open water
in all directions. This central Pacific location means Hawaii
receives swells generated by storms thousands of miles away
in both the North and South Pacific Ocean.

The Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean on Earth covering
approximately 165 million square kilometers. This vast expanse
allows swells to travel enormous distances building energy and
organizing into long period groundswells before reaching Hawaii.
Long period swells with wave periods of 14-18 seconds carry
significantly more energy than shorter period wind waves.

SWELL SOURCES
-------------
North Pacific Swells (Winter — October to April):
Generated by powerful low pressure systems and storms in the
North Pacific Ocean near Alaska and the Aleutian Islands.
These storms can generate massive swells that travel thousands
of miles to reach Hawaii's north and west facing shores.
Winter is the peak big wave season in Hawaii.

South Pacific Swells (Summer — April to October):
Generated by storms in the Southern Ocean near Antarctica.
These long traveling swells wrap around the islands and
reach the south facing shores including Waikiki. South swells
give Waikiki its summer surf season.

Trade Wind Waves:
Hawaii's consistent northeast trade winds generate local wind
waves year round contributing to Waikiki's high surfable
frequency of 48.87%.

SEISMIC ACTIVITY
----------------
Hawaii sits on the Pacific Plate near active volcanic and
seismic zones. The Hawaiian Islands were formed by a hotspot
in the Pacific Plate and volcanic activity continues today
on the Big Island. This geological activity contributes to
moderate seismic activity near Hawaii.

253 earthquake events above magnitude 4.0 were recorded within
500km during the study period. The maximum magnitude recorded
was 6.9. While Hawaii's seismic activity is significant it
is not near major subduction zones like the Pacific Northwest
or Japan which generate the largest tsunamis.

SURF HISTORY
------------
Waikiki is considered the birthplace of modern surfing. Ancient
Hawaiians surfed these waves for centuries and Duke Kahanamoku
introduced surfing to the world from Waikiki beach in the early
1900s. The gentle consistent waves of Waikiki have made it
the most famous beginner surf spot in the world while nearby
locations like Pipeline and Jaws offer world class big wave
surfing.

WHY WAIKIKI RANKS #1
---------------------
Waikiki's top ranking comes from its exceptional combination
of consistent surfable frequency (48.87% — highest of all
4 locations) and moderate seismic activity (30.48 seismic
score). While it doesn't have the highest single wave event
its consistency of surfable conditions year round driven by
consistent Pacific swells from both hemispheres gives it
the highest overall surf potential score.
